# The 0.23 redesign

Date: 2026-11-04 · Version: 0.23.0

This records why the app is shaped the way it is after 0.23. The brand itself
is unchanged. Burgundy, copper, sand, Inter and map-first are commitments in
`documents/brand-guidelines/`, and the redesign holds to them. What changed is
the structure: what is a destination, what is an action, what is a view of
something else, and how much chrome sits around the content.

## Who it is for, and what they are doing

- **A worker** opens the app to find paid work they can reach, decide whether
  to offer, and later to find out whether they were chosen. They look at it
  outdoors, one-handed, often on an inexpensive phone with weak signal.
- **A hirer** opens it to describe a job (often by voice), compare the offers
  that arrive, choose one, and arrange the work. Or, in Mode B, to book a
  listed professional at a fixed price.

Both visit often and briefly. The surface is a tool (Impeccable's *Operate*
mode): it should be quick to scan, act the same everywhere, and keep its
personality in the details.

## What was wrong

Audited on the running build at 390×844 and 1440×900, as the hirer, the worker
and a first-time visitor.

1. **Map and Jobs were two destinations showing the same jobs.** They shared
   one filter state, and on a desktop the map already had the list beside it,
   so the Jobs destination repeated the map's own sidebar.
2. **Activity had five tabs that scrolled.** On a phone, *Offers* (where a
   worker learns whether they were chosen) sat off the right edge with nothing
   to say it was there. The tabs also mixed things that arrive (updates,
   messages) with lists you go and check (saved, posted, offers).
3. **The "Post a Job" button floated over content** on four destinations and
   covered the last rows of every list.
4. **"Ask Trust Hire" used a pair of speech bubbles**, which is what Messages
   looks like. Two features shared one symbol.
5. **On their own posting, a hirer saw "Call this off" above the offers.** A
   red, full-width, destructive button was the first thing on the screen, ahead
   of the task they came to do: choose someone.
6. **Everything was a bordered card**: job rows, offers, settings, notices,
   the map preview. Without hierarchy between containers, nothing stood out.
7. **Wide windows stretched the first-run screens**, so a 1400px "Next" button
   ran under a short sentence. The rail also had no name on it.
8. **The posting form's trade grid shrank labels below 12px** ("Appliance
   repair", "Legal advice") to fit the tiles.
9. **Profile opened with the demo account switcher**, then a role picker drawn
   as two large cards, then settings. Who you are came third.

## Decisions

### Navigation: four destinations and one action

```
 compact (bottom bar)                    medium/expanded (rail)
┌──────┬───────────┬──────┬──────────┬─────────┐    Trust Hire
│ Jobs │ Directory │ Post │ Activity │ Profile │    [+ Post a Job]
└──────┴───────────┴──────┴──────────┴─────────┘    Jobs · Directory · Activity · Profile
```

- **Jobs** is one destination with two views of the same results: a map and a
  list. Switching between them changes how the results are shown, not where
  you are, so it is a toggle inside the destination. Google Maps and Airbnb
  handle map and list results the same way. The map stays the default. On a
  desktop both views are shown at once, so the toggle is not drawn.
- **Post** is an action, so it is drawn as a button, not as a tab. It sits in
  the middle of the bar the way Instagram's create button does: always one tap
  away, never selected, and never on top of content. On the rail it is the
  first thing under the product's name.
- **Activity's** badge now counts unanswered messages as well as unseen
  updates. Before, a new message raised no badge at all.

### The Jobs header: search first

The map's title bar and the list's app bar are now one search bar, floating on
the map and pinned above the list. It holds search, the account avatar and a
labelled **Ask** button. The filter chips sit under it in both views, so the
filters stay where you left them when you switch.

- Ask is a word, not an icon. It is a different way of searching, so it sits
  inside the search bar on both Jobs and Directory.
- The result count moved onto the view toggle ("16 jobs", or "3 of 16 jobs"
  when filtered). It sits where the eye goes to change views.

### Activity: three tabs, one of them filtered

`Updates · Messages · Your jobs`. Updates and messages arrive; your jobs are
checked. Inside *Your jobs*, a segmented control picks **Posted**, **Offers**
or **Saved**, each with a count. These are three subsets of one list of jobs,
which is the job a segmented control does. It opens on Posted for a hirer and
on Offers for a worker.

### A job: the task first, the danger last

On a hirer's own posting, the offers come straight after the job's details.
Edit, Call this off and Delete are in a **More** menu (⋯) in the sheet's
header, with the destructive two marked in red and still confirmed in a
dialogue. Progress actions a worker or hirer needs (Confirm arrival, Mark
finished, Rate) stay as buttons, because they are the next step. Contact is
not offered to the person who posted the number.

### Rows, not cards

A list of jobs is a list: rows on the page ground, separated by hairlines,
with the fare opposite the title. Cards are kept for things that sit *above*
something else: the map's preview, a sheet, a grouped settings block. The
selected row in the desktop list gets a sand fill and a burgundy title. Its
"Open details" button appears on the selected row only, not on every row.

### The ground

App bars sit on the same mist ground as the page beneath them, separated by a
hairline once content scrolls under. Before, a white bar sat over a
grey-pink page over white cards: three surfaces where there is one thing.
White now means "this sits above the page": sheets, menus, the map overlays
and the grouped settings.

### Smaller fixes

- First-run screens are held to a 560px column on wide windows.
- The rail carries the product name.
- Trade tiles wrap their label onto two lines instead of shrinking it. Nothing
  in the app is set below 12px.
- Profile leads with who you are: avatar, name, area, and **Switch account**
  in words under them. The "Demo accounts" heading and the card around the
  person are gone. *Looking for work / Hiring someone* stays as two large
  choices rather than a segmented control. That was decided in 0.20 for a
  reason that still holds: both labels are whole phrases, and in Urdu at a
  large text size a segmented control wrapped them a word per line.
- Empty states lose the 88px sand medallion: a smaller icon, a title, the next
  step, and a button when there is one.

## What was kept, deliberately

- The palette, type scale, radii and touch targets in `lib/core/tokens.dart`.
  They are the brand guidelines' section 31, and the audit found nothing wrong
  with them, only with how they were applied.
- The bottom sheet for job details on a phone. It keeps the map underneath,
  which is what a person comparing nearby jobs wants.
- Every rule, string and refusal the tests protect. The redesign moves
  controls; it does not change what any of them does.

## How it was checked

- The running web build was driven through Flutter's semantics tree in
  headless Chromium at 390×844, 800×600 and 1440×900, light and dark, as each
  persona, before and after.
- `flutter analyze` and `flutter test`, including the six-shape
  `surfaces_test.dart` matrix and the accessibility audit.

## The independent review, and what came of it

After round two, Impeccable's finish reviewer was given the brief, this
document and nineteen screenshots (phone, desktop, dark), with no access to
the conversation that built it. Its verdict was *fix*, not *ship*. What it
found and what was done, in 0.23.2:

| Finding | Done |
| --- | --- |
| The desktop list was headed "Work on this map" above a map showing one city of its results | The count is the heading ("16 jobs", "3 of 16 jobs") |
| On a phone, the trades notice and a pin's preview together left a 200px strip of map | Notices step aside while a preview is open |
| The list view had no count once the switch said "Map" | The count heads the list too |
| Four postings in four states looked identical under *Posted* | Each says its status, and while open how many offers wait ("2 offers", in burgundy) |
| A bookmark on your own posting | Only on other people's jobs |
| Location, time and distance set at 12px with wide tracking | 14px, the size section 11 names for exactly those facts; every style's tracking set to Inter's own (0), where Material's defaults had been filling the gap |
| "★" typed as a character in ratings | A drawn star, from the same icon family as everything else |
| The Directory headed "Book a professional" under a tab named Directory | Headed as the tab is; the sentence under it says what the mode is |
| Directory categories past the window's edge could not be reached with a mouse | Chip rows drag with a mouse or trackpad |
| The intro draws a fare over its pin; the real map never did | The selected pin carries its fare |
| The quiet status pill sat on Material's lavender default | The brand's warm card-border neutral |

Declined, with the reason:

- **A root `PRODUCT.md`, and a concept-roll seed in a direction contract.**
  This repository keeps documentation under `documents/`, and the roll is
  Impeccable's step for *replacing* a visual world. This redesign kept the
  brand on purpose (see the top of this document).
- **"Save Job" → "Post job", and sentence case on every action.** Section 21
  of the brand guidelines lists "Post a Job", "Save Job" and "Edit Job" as the
  product's calls to action, in those words.
- **Copper on the "Your posting" badge.** Section 5 gives copper to new-job
  badges as well as voice notes.
- **A full-width indicator on the desktop rail.** The pill-sized indicator is
  Material 3's own rail, and the app follows Material on every other control.
