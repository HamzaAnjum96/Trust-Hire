## Platform

android

One Flutter app, Material throughout, built for Android phones first and also
shipped to the web (GitHub Pages) and iOS. It does not change its design
language per platform.

## Users

- **Workers**: plumbers, electricians, cleaners, drivers, tutors, cooks,
  helpers across Pakistan. They find paid work they can reach, offer a fare,
  and learn whether they were chosen.
- **Hirers**: households and small businesses who describe a job (often by
  voice note), compare offers, choose one, and arrange the work; or book a
  listed professional at a fixed price.
- **Trust Hire staff**: one admin account that approves listings, settles
  disputes and overrides, with every action logged.

Both main groups visit often and briefly. *(inferred)* Many read slowly or
prefer Urdu, use inexpensive Android phones on weak or metered connections,
and use the app outdoors, one-handed, in bright light.

## Product Purpose

Nearby work, made simple: a map of work happening near you, where a job can be
posted with a voice note, a photo or a few words, without an account or a
form. Success is a hire that happens: a job posted, an offer chosen at a fare
both sides trust, the work done, and both people rated.

## Positioning

A location-first marketplace that treats voice as a first-class way to
describe work, reveals exact locations only to the two people who agreed to
work together, and keeps the platform's own rules (a fare locked at
acceptance, a 5% commission, a debt lockout) visible and enforced rather than
buried.

## Operating Context

- Two modes of hiring: **Mode A**, the map: a hirer posts, workers offer, the
  hirer chooses and the fare locks; **Mode B**, the directory: a paid listing
  with fixed prices, booked directly.
- Offline-first: everything works on the device; a sync queue reconciles
  later. Map tiles, Stripe's checkout and the optional on-device AI are the
  only network uses.
- English and Urdu, Urdu right to left.
- A demo with seven switchable accounts, walked through in
  `documents/product/demo-script.md`.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- No sign-in in the proof of concept; demo accounts stand in.
- Payments are Stripe test mode through Payment Links on the deployed site;
  simulated top-ups elsewhere.
- An on-device AI (WebLLM) answers "Ask Trust Hire" and suggests fares, only
  after the person agrees to a download of stated size.
- Nothing required on a job: a voice note alone, a photo alone or a title
  alone is postable.
- 44–48px touch targets, 16px body text, nothing under 12px, AA contrast, in
  both themes and both languages (enforced by tests).

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Trust Hire**, two words.
- Voice: a helpful person, not a recruitment department. Plain words, no
  jargon ("job", "work", "nearby", "voice note"), recovery steps when
  something fails, optional things genuinely optional.
- Not to look like: a recruitment site, a government portal, a banking app, a
  generic AI startup, a social media clone, or a green-and-white
  "Pakistan-themed" product; no cultural symbols used to signal "Pakistan".
- The palette is **not** binding: the owner has commissioned a reskin
  ("No need to stick to the current branding style", 2026-11-17).

## Evidence on Hand

- Synthetic seed data: 183 jobs across 25 cities, seven demo accounts, offers,
  ratings and ledgers (`code/frontend/assets/seed/`), generated by a script.
- Placeholder job photos and voice notes, generated and labelled as such.
- No real users, testimonials, metrics or photographs. None may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. Location first: work is shown where it is, approximately, until two people
   agree to work together.
2. Nothing required: voice, a photo or a few words is enough.
3. Say what is true, and what a check does not establish.
4. The rules that decide money are enforced, visible and the same for
   everybody.
5. Declining a permission costs sorting, never access.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes; 44–48px targets; full Urdu, right to
left; a job described only by voice says so; colour never the only signal;
reduced motion respected. *(inferred)* Legibility for slow readers and for
bright outdoor light matters more here than density.
